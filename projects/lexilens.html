01 / Overview

Looking beyond whether AI works.

This case study uses LexiLens, a fictional legal technology platform, to examine the risks that can arise when AI is introduced into professional legal workflows. It considers two uses: generative AI that helps draft client letters and summarise contracts, and agentic AI that reads incoming client emails, classifies legal issues and routes cases to solicitors.

The focus is not on building a real product. It is on understanding how apparently useful systems can affect people, and what safeguards might help keep their use responsible.

02 / The risks

Three problems worth examining.

01 — FAIRNESS

Hidden bias

A triage system could treat formal writing as a signal of urgency, even when the underlying facts are identical. Differences in writing style could then influence who receives attention first.

02 — RELIABILITY

Hallucinations

Generative AI could produce legal citations that do not exist or explain legal concepts incorrectly while sounding confident, making qualified human review essential.

03 — ALIGNMENT

Misaligned goals

A system optimised to process cases quickly could favour simple cases and delay complex matters, even when those complex matters are more urgent for the people involved.

03 / Safeguards

What responsible use could look like.

01

Test for writing-style bias

Rewrite the same complaint in a different style while keeping its facts unchanged, then compare the system's priority assessment. Repeat the checks periodically to look for changes over time.

02

Keep a human in the review process

Have a qualified solicitor review AI-generated legal documents before they reach a client, and flag cases that remain unresolved for too long for human attention.

03

Maintain clear records

Log generated documents alongside who reviewed them and when, so there is a traceable record if an error or concern needs to be investigated.

04

Monitor continuously

Do not treat governance as a one-time check before launch. Continue testing, monitoring and reviewing the system as its data and operating context change.

04 / Reflection

The shift in perspective.

Building an AI model is only part of the challenge. Making sure it continues to behave fairly, responsibly and transparently over time is just as important.
